The Process, Step by Step

Start at least 8–12 months before your program begins. Processing times vary by country.

  1. 01

    Choose a program and a DLI

    Pick a school that is a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) — only DLIs can host international students. Check the program's eligibility for a Post-Graduation Work Permit if you plan to work after you finish.

  2. 02

    Meet admission requirements and apply

    Submit transcripts, language test results (IELTS, CELPIP, TOEFL, PTE or TEF/TCF for French) and any other documents your school asks for. Pay the application fee.

  3. 03

    Get your Letter of Acceptance & PAL/TAL

    Once admitted, you receive a Letter of Acceptance (LOA). Most students also need a Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letter (PAL/TAL), usually issued by your school. Graduate (master's and PhD) students are generally exempt.

  4. 04

    Prepare proof of funds

    Show you can pay your first-year tuition, living costs and travel. Common proof: a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC), bank statements, scholarship letters, or an education loan.

  5. 05

    Apply for a study permit online

    Apply through your IRCC account. Upload your documents, pay fees, and give biometrics (fingerprints and photo) at a Visa Application Centre when asked. A medical exam may be required.

  6. 06

    Receive your approval

    If approved, you get a Port of Entry (POE) Letter of Introduction and, if needed, a visitor visa (TRV) or eTA. Your actual study permit is issued when you arrive in Canada.

  7. 07

    Travel and arrive

    Bring your documents in your carry-on. A border services officer will review them and issue your study permit. Check every detail on it before leaving the counter.

Information & Documents You'll Need

Identity

  • Valid passport (ideally valid for your full program)
  • Digital passport-style photos
  • Birth certificate (some cases)

Admission

  • Letter of Acceptance from a DLI
  • PAL/TAL (unless exempt)
  • CAQ — if studying in Quebec
  • Academic transcripts & diplomas
  • Language test results

Finances

  • Proof of tuition payment or funds
  • GIC certificate or bank statements
  • Scholarship / sponsor letters
  • Loan approval letter (if any)

Application extras

  • Statement of purpose / study plan
  • Biometrics
  • Medical exam results (if required)
  • Police certificate (if required)
  • Family information form

When You Arrive

At the airport

Show your passport, POE letter, LOA, proof of funds and PAL. Get your study permit and confirm the conditions (work authorization, expiry date).

First week

Apply for a Social Insurance Number (SIN), open a bank account, get a Canadian phone plan and arrange health coverage.

Settling in

Attend orientation, register for classes, get your student ID and transit pass, and connect with your school's international student office.

Work While You Study

  • Up to 24 hours per week off campus during academic sessions (if your permit allows)
  • Full-time during scheduled breaks such as summer and winter holidays
  • On-campus work with no hourly limit in most cases
  • Co-op and internship placements need a co-op work permit

After Graduation

  • Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) — up to 3 years depending on program length
  • Canadian work experience can help you qualify for permanent residence through Express Entry or Provincial Nominee Programs
  • Build in-demand skills and a professional network early

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a study permit take to process?

It depends on your country of residence — from a few weeks to several months. Check current processing times on the IRCC website and apply early.

Can my spouse or children come with me?

Some family members may be eligible for open work permits or study permits, depending on your program level. Rules changed in 2024, so check current eligibility.

Do I need a visa as well as a study permit?

A study permit is not a visa. Depending on your citizenship you'll also need a visitor visa (TRV) or an eTA — normally issued automatically with your study permit approval.

Can I change schools after arriving?

Yes, but your new school must be a DLI, and in many cases you'll need to apply for a new study permit before you transfer.

What if my study permit is expiring?

Apply to extend it from inside Canada before it expires. If you applied in time you can usually keep studying while you wait for a decision.
